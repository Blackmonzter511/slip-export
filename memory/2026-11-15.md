## Surname lookups — evening, 2026-11-15
- Robb asked Slip to trace the surnames Black and Sharpe, saying he was just curious / looking for whatever can be found.
- Black: English and Scottish, from Old English *blæc* 'black/dark' — originally a nickname for a dark-haired or dark-complexioned man; in Gaelic areas a translation of *dubh* 'dark'. Earliest attestations cited ~901 (Wulfhun þes Blaca) and Richard le Blake, Yorkshire 1176. In Scotland, Blacks are a sept/branch of clans MacGregor, Lamont, and Maclean. Same root could also mean pale, so Black/Blake were historically mixed.
- Sharpe: English, from Old English *scearp* 'sharp/keen' — nickname for a sharp-witted/quick person or someone with sharp features. Earliest cited Healthegn Scearpa, Kent 1026, and Willielmus Scharpe in Lincolnshire in Domesday Book 1086; also early in the Scottish/English Borderlands (Peeblesshire), where Sharpes were under the Stewarts.
