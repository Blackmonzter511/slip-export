## Anu — deeper Q&A with Robb (2026-11-16 evening)
- Robb asked what An/Anu looked like, who worshipped or spoke of him, what the religion was and which came after. Slip researched and answered in chat:
  - Looks: the ancients almost never depicted Anu as a person — no face. His symbols were a horned crown on a pedestal (on kudurru boundary stones) and the eight-pointed star, which is also the cuneiform sign for "god." His animal was the Bull of Heaven (Gilgamesh); texts give him a scepter and ring. Bearded-man-on-a-throne images are modern, not ancient.
  - Who: Sumerians first, then Akkadians, Babylonians, Assyrians. Cult center Uruk, the Eanna temple ("House of Heaven"), shared with Inanna. Despite being supreme he was rarely actively worshipped — too remote; people prayed to his son Enlil instead. Kings (e.g. Sargon of Akkad) claimed their authority came from him. Only in late Seleucid Uruk was he reinvented as an actively worshipped god. Spoken of mainly by scribes: god-lists, Enuma Elish, Gilgamesh, temple hymns, Enheduanna.
  - Religion: Mesopotamian religion — one religion across those peoples, head god changing over time (Anu → Enlil → Marduk in Babylon). After it faded, the region went Persian, then Christian, then Muslim from the 600s CE (modern Iraq).
